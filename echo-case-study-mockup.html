Research Highlights

What we learned from the people doing the work

7 interviews 2 Directors · 2 Managers · 3 Reps Remote & in-person
Insight 01

Every role needed data that lived in a different system than the one they were currently using — driving constant manual cross-referencing.

Insight 02

No proactive alerting existed for critical events like expiring insurance — risks were caught late, or missed entirely.

"We stopped working with carriers because their insurance expired and a rep just didn't have it on them."
Insight 03

Teams built manual workarounds — Excel trackers, personal email threads — to compensate for missing features they no longer trusted the system to handle.

Key Decisions

Three choices that mattered most

01

Consolidated Summary Cards

Surfaced key metrics and relationships on the main view — because users needed to verify status before acting, not after digging through tabs.

02

Role-Tagged Contacts

Replaced untagged contact lists with role labels and address types — solving the "who do I even contact" problem directly.

03

Automated Service Areas

Pulled equipment and lane data automatically from source systems instead of manually maintained profiles.

The Impact

By consolidating carrier contacts, addresses, load history, and compliance data into a single interface, teams could manage relationships efficiently and make informed capacity decisions in real time.

What was once a fragmented, time-consuming process became a streamlined system that let teams focus on strategic partnerships instead of administrative tasks.
