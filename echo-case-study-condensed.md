# Echo Global Logistics — Carrier Profile Management
### Condensed Case Study (Optimized for Skimming)

---

## Overview

**Role:** Senior UX Designer · **Timeline:** 2025 · **Services:** Strategy, Research, Design · **Platform:** Web Application

Unifying workflows across legacy platforms to improve efficiency for supply chain professionals.

---

## The Problem

Carrier teams relied on **three disconnected legacy tools** — Clutch, Optimizer, and STATS — to do a single job. None of them shared data automatically.

> *"There's a lot of different places where you need to do one thing to do another."*

**[VISUAL: Before/After Comparison]**
- **Before:** Screenshot of legacy Echo Workplace profile (dense, dated UI, all fields flat with no hierarchy)
- **After:** Screenshot of new Echo Accelerator Summary tab (card-based, scannable, prioritized info)
- *Caption: "3 disconnected systems → 1 unified carrier profile"*

This fragmentation created real business risk — missed insurance expirations, inconsistent carrier data, and slower, less accurate capacity matching.

---

## Research Highlights

**Method:** 7 interviews (2 Directors, 2 Managers, 3 Reps), synthesized into a research canvas separating what we *did* from what we *learned*.

Instead of one generic user flow, I mapped **what each role actually used each tool for** — revealing that every person needed data that lived in a system they weren't currently working in.

**Three insights that shaped the design:**

> **Insight 1:** Every role needed data that lived in a different system than the one they were currently using — driving constant manual cross-referencing.

> **Insight 2:** There was no proactive alerting for critical events (like expiring insurance), so risks were only caught after the fact — or missed entirely.
> *"We stopped working with carriers because their insurance expired and a rep just didn't have it on them."*

> **Insight 3:** Teams had built manual workarounds (Excel trackers, personal email threads) to compensate for missing product features — a sign the system wasn't trusted to do the job.

## Process

Design went through 4 rounds of iteration on the core Summary layout before landing on the final structure.

**[VISUAL: Wireframe Evolution Strip — 4 thumbnails side by side]**
- **v1:** Flat tab structure, no prioritization
- **v2:** Added Score Card + Relationships panel
- **v3:** Added Available Equipment / Preferred Areas / Insights cards
- **v4:** Split Compliance Notes and Carrier Notes into dedicated panels

Each design pass was reviewed with annotated questions directly on the wireframes, keeping decisions traceable to research and surfacing open questions before development — e.g., *"Is this the right placement for viewing preferred lanes?"*

---

## Key Decisions

**1. Consolidated Summary Cards**
Surfaced Score Card metrics and Related Profiles directly on the main view instead of behind separate tabs — because Directors and Managers needed to verify carrier status *before* deciding to act, not after digging through tabs.

**2. Role-Tagged Contacts & Categorized Addresses**
Replaced untagged contact lists with role labels (Dispatch, Accounting, Driver) and address types (Corporate, Remit, Terminal) — directly solving the "who do I even contact for this" problem from research.

**3. Automated, Integrated Service Areas**
Pulled equipment and lane data automatically from RMIS and EchoDrive instead of relying on manually maintained profiles — fixing the root cause of outdated capacity-matching data.

*(3 additional feature decisions — Load History, Documents, Compliance workflows — covered in the extended case study.)*

---

## The Impact

The unified carrier management system transformed how teams maintained carrier relationships and managed operational workflows.

By consolidating carrier contacts, addresses, load history, and compliance data into a single interface, teams could manage relationships efficiently and make informed capacity decisions in real time — automated compliance workflows reduced manual exception handling, while integrated data improved load-matching accuracy.

What was once a fragmented, time-consuming process became a streamlined system that let teams focus on strategic partnerships instead of administrative tasks.
